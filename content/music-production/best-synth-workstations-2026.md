## Overview

The Yamaha MODX M8 is my reference point here, so this is a comparison of the other serious 88-key workstations you might cross-shop against it: the **Korg Nautilus**, the **Roland Fantom 8 EX** and the **Kurzweil K2700**. I'm leaving digital pianos for a separate post, because that is a different buying decision.

A note on prices: street prices for all of these vary a lot by retailer and region, so I only quote launch prices that I could source and tell you where I couldn't.

---

## Yamaha MODX M8

- Launched in October 2025 as part of the MODX M range (M6, M7 and M8). Sound On Sound reported US launch prices of $2,199 / $2,499 / $3,099 for the 61, 76 and 88-key models.
- Three engines: **AN-X** (virtual analogue), **FM-X** and **AWM2** (sampled instruments).
- Eight physical faders and a Super Knob for multi-parameter modulation.
- A free virtual version, the Expanded Softsynth Plugin (E.S.P.), was announced for early 2026 for registered users.
- 88 keys with Yamaha's GHS action (no aftertouch) and a 7-inch colour touchscreen, per Yamaha's spec pages.
- Up to 268 notes of polyphony across the three engines (128 AWM2, 128 FM-X, 12 AN-X). Yamaha's US and European pages disagree slightly on the sample memory figure, so I've left it out.
- Positioned as a more compact, lighter and more affordable take on the Montage M flagship.

See my [MODX M8 vs Montage M8X](/music-production/modx-m8-vs-montage-m8x/) comparison for how it differs from its bigger sibling.

## Korg Nautilus

- Available in 88, 73 and 61-key versions. The 88 has Korg's RH-3 weighted hammer action.
- Korg advertises nine dedicated sound engines. Its page names seven, including SGX-2 piano, EP-1 electric piano, CX-3 organ, MOD-7 (VPM/FM), PolysixEX and MS-20EX (analogue modelling) and STR-1 (physical modelling).
- 8-inch colour touchscreen, 16 MIDI tracks plus 16 audio tracks, and 16 effects processors.
- Open Sampling System: it can sample external audio or resample itself, and loads AIFF, WAV, SoundFont 2.0 and AKAI S1000/3000 files.
- Korg announced it in late 2020 at US prices of about $2,700 (88-key), according to MusicTech. Current street prices vary, so check a dealer.

---

## Roland Fantom 8 EX

- Announced in April 2024 as the FANTOM 6 EX, 7 EX and 8 EX. The 8 EX has 88 hammer-action keys and all models support aftertouch.
- Engines: ACB (Analog Circuit Behavior), ZEN-Core, V-Piano, SuperNATURAL and Virtual ToneWheel, with over 7,000 sounds and eleven expansions included, among them JUPITER-8, SH-101 and JX-3P ACB models.
- Colour touchscreen, onboard sampling, a TR-REC step sequencer and 16 RGB pads for triggering sounds, samples and clips.
- US launch prices were $3,199.99 (6 EX), $3,699.99 (7 EX) and $4,199.99 (8 EX).
- Owners of the original Fantom 6/7/8 can add the EX features through the paid FANTOM EX Upgrade on Roland Cloud.

I've also written about the [Roland Jupiter-X](/music-production/roland-jupiter-x/) and the older [Fantom EX](/music-production/roland-fantom-ex/).

## Kurzweil K2700

- 88 fully weighted keys, over 60 physical controllers (ribbon, drum pads, sliders, knobs), and a built-in audio interface.
- V.A.S.T. synthesis alongside a virtual analogue engine and a six-operator FM engine, with 256 voices of V.A.S.T. polyphony and over 1,500 factory programs.
- MusicRadar's review praises the real-time control and multiple tone generators, but says some sounds feel dated and the screen is not a touchscreen.
- You can load up to 3.5 GB of your own samples (WAV, AIF) into flash memory, per Kurzweil.
- Retail prices I found ranged from roughly $2,500 to $3,300. I couldn't find an official list price.

## My Take

*This section is my own opinion, not tested reporting. I have not played the Nautilus, Fantom 8 EX or K2700 side by side with the MODX M8.*

- **Sound design and expressive playing:** the MODX M8's three-engine design and faders make it the one I would reach for. It is what I own and use, so I am biased.
- **Breadth of sounds in one box:** the Nautilus, with its range of engines and sampling, looks like the broadest option for the money.
- **Stage and groove-based production:** the Fantom 8 EX leans toward pads, step sequencing and vintage Roland character, but it is the most expensive at launch.
- **Hands-on control:** the K2700 has the most physical controls, but the sounds and screen feel older.
