## Overview

This is the companion to my [synth workstation comparison](/music-production/best-synth-workstations-2026/). A workstation like the MODX M8 is about sound design and layering. These five are about the piano itself: the key action, the piano sound and, for some of them, whether you need to carry it up a flight of stairs.

The five are a shortlist drawn from reviews I read and the manufacturers' own spec pages, not a ranking from testing them myself. I have not played all of them side by side. I've left prices out because they vary too much by retailer and region.

---

## 1. Nord Piano 6

- 88-key Triple Sensor hammer action with three touch curves, and a weight of 19.2 kg (42.3 lb), per Nord's spec page.
- Two independent piano layers with 120-voice polyphony, and 2 GB of memory for piano sounds.
- Sounds come from the Nord Piano Library (grand, upright, electric, digital, clav/harpsichord), which can be swapped or expanded through the Nord Sound Manager.
- MusicRadar names it its top overall pick in its stage piano guide. I couldn't open the full article, so I'm relying on the summary for that.

---

## 2. Yamaha CP88

- 88 keys with Yamaha's NW-GH action (Natural Wood Graded Hammer) and synthetic ebony and ivory keytops. Yamaha's regional pages call it both NW-GH and NW-GH3.
- 128 notes of polyphony, 18.6 kg (41 lb), and no built-in speakers.
- Pianist's Compass picks it for "best vintage sound" and notes it has fewer sounds than some rivals.

---

## 3. Roland RD-2000 EX

- 88-key PHA-50 hybrid action with escapement and ebony/ivory feel. Roland says it's the same keyboard used in its premium grand pianos.
- Two independent engines: V-Piano modelling and SuperNATURAL (128 voices).
- 21.7 kg (about 48 lb), no built-in speakers, and a 24-bit/192 kHz USB audio/MIDI interface.
- The 2024 update to the 2017 RD-2000, announced in July 2024.
- Pianist's Compass picks it as "best for performers".

---

## 4. Kawai ES920

- Responsive Hammer III action: 88 grade-weighted keys, Ivory Touch key surfaces, let-off simulation and triple-sensor detection.
- Built-in speakers: two 8 x 12 cm full-range drivers, 40 W total.
- 256 notes of polyphony and 38 sounds, built on 88-key stereo sampling (Harmonic Imaging XL).
- 17.0 kg (37.5 lb) without the music rest, the lightest of the four whose weight I could confirm.

---

## 5. Yamaha Clavinova CLP-835

- A home piano in a cabinet rather than a stage instrument. 88 weighted keys with GrandTouch-S action, per Yamaha.
- Binaural CFX piano sound with Virtual Resonance Modeling and a built-in speaker system.
- I couldn't find its polyphony or weight in the sources I checked.
- Pianist's Compass picks it as "best for home" and lists cost as the drawback.

---

## My Take

*This section is my own opinion. I have not tested these side by side.*

- **If you already own a workstation:** a stage piano like the CP88 or Nord Piano 6 is the better partner, because you want a dedicated piano and don't need more synth engines.
- **If you want one instrument with speakers:** the Kawai ES920 has built-in speakers, which the CP88 and RD-2000 EX lack.
- **If it will live in a room:** the CLP-835 is a piece of furniture, and the key feel and cabinet are the reason to buy it.
