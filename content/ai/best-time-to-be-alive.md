This is a personal post. No benchmarks, no announcements, just how it feels from where I'm sitting.

And from where I'm sitting, this is the best time to be alive.

I don't say that lightly. I've been around computers long enough to remember when getting anything meaningful done meant reading manuals cover to cover, waiting for the right person to have time for you, or simply accepting that some ideas would stay ideas forever. Most of mine did. Not because they were bad ideas, but because there were only so many hours, and only so many skills one person could realistically hold.

That ceiling has moved. A lot.

## Finally Fixing the Way I Work

The first thing I noticed wasn't some grand creative breakthrough. It was the small stuff.

Both my work life and my personal life are full of friction - repetitive tasks, half-built systems, notes scattered everywhere, things I knew could be better but never had the time to fix. For years, improving my own workflow was always the thing that got pushed to "one day".

Now it actually gets done. I describe what's annoying me, an agent helps me build the fix, and I move on. The improvements compound. Each small automation frees up a bit more time and attention, which goes into the next improvement. I wrote about one version of this in [my AI-augmented design workflow](/ai/ai-augmented-design-workflow/), but it's spread well beyond work now.

## Running Family Life

It's not just work. AI has quietly become one of the most useful tools I have for managing family affairs.

Children, school, health, finances, legal advice - the admin of family life never stops, and a lot of it involves wading through information that's either confusing, scattered or written for someone else. Now I can talk it through, get my bearings quickly, and work out what actually matters before deciding what to do next.

It's also changed how I buy things. Product comparisons, shortlisting the options that actually fit what we need, establishing the top 10 of something - jobs that used to swallow whole evenings of tabs and reviews now take a fraction of the time, and I come out the other side feeling far more confident in the decision.

## Feeling Like I Can Build Anything

For the first time, I genuinely feel like I can build anything. The distance between "I wish this existed" and "here it is, working" has collapsed, and that changes how I think about every problem in front of me.

## Turning Melodies Into Finished Tracks

Music has always been where this hits hardest for me.

I [play intuitively](/music-production/why-i-play-and-create-music-intuitively/) - by ear, chasing the moments where my hands find something I didn't plan. Those moments produce a lot of musical phrases and melodies. Historically, most of them never went anywhere. Turning a sixteen-bar idea into a finished, arranged, mixed track is a completely different skill set from finding the idea in the first place, and it's the part where I'd usually stall.

Now I can take those phrases and melodies and turn them into complete tracks and songs. The idea is still mine. The feel is still mine. But the long, grinding distance between a spark and a finished piece is no longer a wall. You can hear where that's taken me in [my tracks](/music-production/my-tracks/).

## Art and Video I Could Never Have Made

I can't draw. I've accepted that for a long time.

What I hadn't expected was that it would stop mattering quite so much. I can now generate artwork and video for my music and my posts - things that would have been simply impossible for me before, unless I paid someone or went without. Having a visual side to ideas that used to be purely sonic or purely technical has changed how I think about them. They feel more complete.

## The Agents Have My Back

Beyond building and creating, there's the thinking.

I use AI to research ideas, philosophies, theories and issues. To weigh up options before making a decision. To argue the other side when I'm too sure of myself. To help me write better articles and posts - including this one, sharpened from a page of rough notes.

It feels like having a tireless research partner and sounding board available whenever curiosity strikes, at whatever hour that happens to be. For someone who has always had more questions than time to answer them, that's an extraordinary thing.

## Sharing It With People Who Care

One of the parts I enjoy most is something I didn't see coming: sharing what I'm learning.

When I show people around me what's possible now - a workflow, a tool, a trick, a track - the reaction is often genuine excitement, and I get a huge amount of positive energy back from it. People appreciate it, and that appreciation feeds straight back into me wanting to learn more and share more. This blog is part of that loop too.

## The Loop Is Speeding Up

My honest impression of the last few weeks is that the models have taken another big step forward. Compared with even a few months ago, my own productivity feels massively higher, everything I use is cheaper and faster, and the ideas are flooding in faster than ever - because when you can act on ideas quickly, you have more of them.

My loop is accelerating. Idea, build, test, learn, next idea - and each cycle is shorter than the last. More and more, I can take myself out of the loop entirely and let agents handle most of the execution, checking in at the points where judgement matters.

## Stepping Back to Think

That shift has changed what my working day actually looks like.

For most of my career, I wrote code all day. Now I spend far more time stepping back and asking how things should *really* work - in an ideal world, not just within the constraints of what I'd have had time to build myself. I focus on ideas and design, and I brainstorm them with agents before anything gets built. It's the shift I described in [Agent-First Architecture: The Engineer as System Curator](/ai/agent-first-architecture-engineer-as-curator/), and living it is even more interesting than writing about it.

It turns out that when execution gets cheap, [taste becomes the scarce thing](/ai/taste-is-the-new-scarcity/). That's a much more enjoyable problem to have.

## Why I Think This Is the Best Time to Be Alive

I believe we're here to learn and to progress. Right now, almost anyone can do almost anything, as long as they have access to these tools and the curiosity to use them.

That's what makes this moment different. The barrier used to be skill, time, money or access to the right people. Increasingly, the barrier is just imagination and the willingness to start. I explored the bigger-picture version of this in [We Are Learning to Buy Intelligence](/ai/we-are-learning-to-buy-intelligence/), but the personal version is simpler: I'm doing things I genuinely thought I'd never get to do.

## The Part I Haven't Stopped Worrying About

None of this means I've stopped worrying.

I'm still concerned about AI safety. The same capabilities that let me build in days what used to take months are moving fast for everyone, and the question of whether we can keep these systems aligned, understandable and under meaningful control is not solved. I've written about [what I think actually matters in AI safety](/ai/ai-safety-first-principles/), and nothing about my excitement changes that. If anything, seeing how quickly things are moving from the inside makes it feel more urgent, not less.

Optimism and concern aren't opposites. I can be amazed by what these tools let me do and still want the people building them to be careful.

## Only Just Getting Started

The strangest part is that it still feels like the beginning. Every few weeks something new becomes possible, and the list of things I want to try grows faster than I can work through it.

The future looks incredibly bright. Let's make sure we get it right.
