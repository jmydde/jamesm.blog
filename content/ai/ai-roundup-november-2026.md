## TL;DR

- **Anthropic** released **Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2** on 9 November, and set a **$70 to $78** price range for its IPO, with pricing reported for 18 November
- **OpenAI** closed the first **$11 billion** of a funding round at a **$1.25 trillion** valuation, according to Reuters and Bloomberg
- **Marvell** agreed to buy **Lumentum** in an all-stock deal valued at **$115 billion**
- **Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO** on 12 November
- Four nonprofits sued **OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX's AI division, Meta and Google** seeking to bar further development of advanced AI models

*This post covers the month up to 13 November and will be updated at the end of the month. Allegations in the lawsuit are the plaintiffs' claims, not established facts.*

## Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 (9 November)

According to [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-fable-5-2-model-details-here/), Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, the second major update to the Fable 5 class after Fable 5.1 in September.

- **Pricing:** $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, which Anthropic says is cheaper than before
- **Claims:** better coding, reasoning and vision, and outputs up to 30% faster than Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1. These figures come from Anthropic and had not been independently tested at the time of reporting
- **Two names, one model:** secondary coverage reports that Fable and Mythos are the same underlying model, and that Mythos is the version available to vetted organisations with fewer cybersecurity safeguards
- **Guidance:** Anthropic recommends Claude Opus 5.5 as the everyday model for most tasks, with Fable or Mythos for the most demanding work

## Anthropic's IPO

An amended filing on 2 November added third-quarter results. According to [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-ipo-filing-17bn-revenue-commitments) and other coverage:

- Q3 revenue was about **$16.96 billion**, and nine-month revenue was **$33.24 billion**
- Quarter-on-quarter growth slowed to about 47%
- Purchase commitments and leases total about **$895.4 billion**, substantially all related to compute and infrastructure
- Sources differ on the size of the Q3 operating loss, and the net loss was driven largely by an accounting charge from revaluing convertible instruments

On 10 November an updated filing set a price range of **$70 to $78 per share** for **825 million shares**, listing on Nasdaq under the ticker **ANT**. [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-sets-price-range-of-70-to-78-in-upcoming-ipo.html), citing people familiar with the plans, that pricing is expected on 18 November and trading on 19 November. Those dates are not in the filing, and the deal has not priced as of this writing. For a builder's reading of the filing, see [Anthropic's IPO: What the S-1 Says That Matters If You Build on Claude](/ai/anthropic-ipo-s-1-what-builders-should-read/).

## OpenAI's $1.25 Trillion Round

Reuters reported on 6 November, citing a source familiar with the matter, that OpenAI had closed an initial **$11 billion** of new funding at a **$1.25 trillion** valuation. Coverage via [DealStreetAsia](https://www.dealstreetasia.com/?p=500415) and others says the round could grow, and earlier talks had targeted more. It is unclear whether the valuation is pre-money or post-money. OpenAI's previous round, announced on 31 March, was at an $852 billion post-money valuation.

## Marvell Buys Lumentum for $115 Billion

Marvell agreed to acquire Lumentum in an all-stock deal with an implied enterprise value of **$115 billion**, according to [Light Reading](https://lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/marvell-strikes-115b-deal-for-lumentum) and other reports. Lumentum holders receive 4.5 Marvell shares per share, and the companies expect Marvell shareholders to own about 67% of the combined business. The deal is subject to shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the first half of 2028.

## Jensen Huang Resigns from Nvidia

On 12 November Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as President, CEO and board member. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and Harvey C. Jones is the board's first chair. The full facts are in [Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia CEO: What We Know](/ai/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia/).

## Lawsuit Seeks to Bar Frontier AI Development

According to a [Reuters report carried by Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/11/13/341283.htm), a group of nonprofits including the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen and Heat Initiative filed suit in San Francisco against OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX's AI division, Meta and Google. They ask for an order barring further development of advanced AI models, citing California's unfair business practices laws and public nuisance claims. These are the plaintiffs' allegations. Meta declined to comment and the other companies had not responded at the time of reporting.

## Agent Tooling: Hermes Agent v0.23

Nous Research released Hermes Agent v0.23.0 on 5 November, adding a browser-based Webapp, a single gateway that owns every conversation, conversation rewind and spend limits for paid tools. The [release notes](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/releases/tag/v0.23.0) have the full list.
