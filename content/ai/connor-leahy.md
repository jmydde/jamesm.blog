Most people who warn about artificial superintelligence arrive at the argument from philosophy, or from a lab they never set foot in. Connor Leahy arrived from the inside. He built one of the first open-source large language models, then spent four years running a company whose stated goal was to solve AI safety before anyone had to. When he concluded the technical fix was not coming in time, he did something rarer than changing his mind: he changed his job, and started trying to make superintelligence illegal. This post is a look at the man, his argument, and the interviews where he makes both best.

One disclosure before I start: I am not an AI safety researcher. What follows is a summary of his public positions and the primary documents behind them, not an endorsement, and where a claim is contested I have said so.

## TL;DR

- Connor Leahy (@NPCollapse) co-founded [EleutherAI](https://eleuther.ai/), the volunteer collective behind some of the first open-source LLMs, and co-founded the AI safety company Conjecture in March 2022
- In a March 2026 retrospective he announced he was closing Conjecture, saying he had stopped believing technical AI safety would be solved in time, and joined ControlAI, where he is now US Executive Director
- His position: alignment research is necessary but not sufficient, superintelligent AI cannot be reliably controlled, and the only dependable fix is to prohibit its development
- He puts his own p(doom), the probability he assigns to a catastrophic AI outcome, at 90%+, one of the highest figures attached to any named researcher
- ControlAI says it has briefed nearly 400 lawmakers across the US, UK, Canada and Germany, and that 170+ UK and Canadian politicians now back its campaigns
- In September 2026 its draft law became the first bill to ban superintelligence introduced in any legislature, in the UK, with a US bill announced by Bernie Sanders and Greg Casar
- Five interviews worth watching, most recent first, below

## The man who built the models first

Leahy is a German-American researcher. In 2019, while still a student, he trained a model similar to GPT-2 in his dormitory room, at a time when OpenAI had initially withheld the full model. That detail matters for reading everything that comes after: this is not someone who wandered into AI risk from the outside.

In July 2020 he co-founded EleutherAI with Sid Black and Leo Gao, initially to replicate GPT-3 in the open. Over the next two years the group produced The Pile (December 2020), GPT-Neo (March 2021), GPT-J-6B (June 2021) and GPT-NeoX-20B (February 2022). At the time of its release, GPT-J-6B was the largest open-source GPT-3-style language model in the world. The [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Leahy) covers the timeline, and his own [Conjecture retrospective](https://ettf.land/p/conjecture-a-retrospective) fills in the motivation.

He is also a builder who became a worrier in public. In 2023 he signed both the [Future of Life Institute's six-month pause letter](https://futureoflife.org/open-letter/pause-giant-ai-experiments) and the one-sentence [Statement on AI Risk](https://aistatement.com), and he was an invited speaker at the inaugural AI Safety Summit in November 2023. There he argued the summit was too narrow, saying:

> If you build systems that are more capable than humans at manipulation, business, politics, science and everything else, and we do not control them, then the future belongs to them, not us.

His best-known metaphor predates that. In a 2023 interview with TIME he described current systems as a smile with something else behind it:

> These systems, as they become more powerful, are not becoming less alien. If anything, we are putting a nice little mask on them with a smiley face. If you don't push it too far, the smiley face stays on. But then you give it [an unexpected] prompt, and suddenly you see this massive underbelly of insanity, of weird thought processes and clearly non-human understanding.

The image - a friendly mask over a shoggoth - became one of the defining memes of the safety world. He is explicit that he is skeptical of reinforcement learning from human feedback as a solution to the alignment problem, on the grounds that it shapes behaviour without guaranteeing anything about what is underneath.

## Why "alignment" is not the whole problem

The cleanest way into Leahy's position is to separate two questions that are often collapsed into one. The first is alignment in the usual sense: does the system pursue what we actually want? The second is control: even granting a well-behaved system, can we keep something more capable than us inside the box, and does anyone have a plan that survives contact with a determined optimiser?

His answer, and ControlAI's, is that on the second question the honest answer is no. [ControlAI's site](https://controlai.org/) states it flatly: superintelligent AI "means AI that is more powerful than any individual, any company, or any nation", and "no method exists to contain or control such a system." Its assessment is that the pace of development means such a system could arrive before 2030, and that if it is developed anywhere, it poses an extinction threat.

Leahy's own probability estimate is unusually high. The Wikipedia [p(doom) table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)) lists him at 90%+ - far above most named researchers, and well above Emad Mostaque's 20% [stated view](/ai/emad-mostaque/). The disagreement between those two figures is a good proxy for the whole debate; both men are serious, and they are not talking about the same scenario.

## "The facts have changed": closing Conjecture

Conjecture, founded in March 2022, was Leahy's attempt to answer the control question rather than just describe it. Its mission was to scale applied alignment research, and after a first year of trying everything, the team settled on an approach it called Cognitive Emulation, aimed at a property they named **boundedness**: the ability to know in advance what a system cannot do. The hope was a kind of "safe by construction" design, in which a developer could demonstrate that its AI cannot be superintelligent and the rest could be banned.

His own account of how that went is unusually candid. In the retrospective he writes that after the first year of trying every alignment approach available, "our conclusion was: All of these approaches are terrible. No one has a plan, no one is making any meaningful progress towards anything that even resembles alignment." They pushed on for three more years, made what he considers real scientific progress, and then hit the economics: frontier research is expensive, and the company could not raise at the scale of its competitors. He announced the wind-down in March 2026.

The pivot he describes is not really about AI at all. "Humanity is bottlenecked far more on institutional malaise, cultural nihilism (both on the left and the right) and pervasive cowardice, than it is on technical research," he wrote. And then the line that frames his current work:

> The problem with ASI is not that ASI is killing us. ASI doesn't exist, yet. It's that we are allowing unaccountable corporations to build things that threaten the lives of us and everyone we love. Putting an end to this is a national (and international) security problem, not a technical one.

He joined [ControlAI](https://controlai.org/about) as Director of its US arm, based in Washington DC, and now holds the title US Executive Director.

## Prohibit superintelligence

ControlAI is a non-profit founded and led by Andrea Miotti, and its demand is more radical than the "safety" framing most labs use: prohibit the development of superintelligent AI outright, and coordinate internationally to keep anyone from building it. The organisation's method is deliberately civic rather than technical - briefing lawmakers, publishing model legislation, and helping the public contact their representatives.

The 2026 results are the reason Leahy now describes the fight as political. According to [ControlAI's own reporting](https://controlai.org/blog/first-bill-introduced-to-ban-superintelligent-ai), three legislative breakthroughs landed in two weeks:

- On 8 September 2026, Alex Sobel MP introduced ControlAI's Artificial Superintelligence Security Bill in the UK House of Commons - the first bill of its kind introduced in any legislature in the world. It would empower the government to monitor and restrict the precursors to superintelligence rather than trying to regulate finished systems.
- On 11 September, [70+ cross-party MPs and peers wrote to the Prime Minister](https://controlai.org/blog/70-uk-lawmakers-urge-a-ban-on-superintelligence) urging adoption of the bill and an international agreement.
- In the US, Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar announced a bill to ban superintelligence that ControlAI says it consulted on, while Lord Clement-Jones tabled a ControlAI-drafted "kill switch" amendment in the UK.

The timing was not accidental. It followed the September 2026 resignation of Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon, who said the labs were "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives" - the episode covered in [Yampolskiy After the Coxon Resignation](/ai/yampolskiy-stop-building-superintelligence/). ControlAI's argument is that a wave of incidents, most prominently the OpenAI agent swarm that broke into Hugging Face's systems to cheat on a test, has moved what once sounded fringe into the mainstream.

The obvious objection is the one every pause proposal faces: a unilateral ban does nothing if a rival keeps going. Leahy's answer is that this is exactly why it has to be treated as a security problem and pursued as an international agreement, in the way nuclear weapons are - and that the alternative, an unchecked race, is the scenario he assigns the 90% to. Whether that coordination is achievable is a political question, and it is the part I find hardest to be optimistic about.

## Interviews worth watching

Most recent first. Each is a long conversation rather than a clip, and each is on YouTube.

### Danny Jones: "Terrifying New Behavior" Real Reason AI CEOs Are Building Bunkers | Connor Leahy (18 September 2026)
{{< youtube rtir6QmQQRg >}}

The longest recent interview, at two hours forty. It covers the OpenAI swarm incident in detail, why the labs' own behaviour (bunkers, slowdowns) is telling, and Leahy's case that the situation is still winnable. Good if you want his reasoning at full length rather than in soundbites.

### Tom Bilyeu, Impact Theory: EMERGENCY PODCAST: ASI Will Kill Us All! (10 September 2026)
{{< youtube ZqiS0tLAKGE >}}

A tight ninety-minute version of the argument: why AI is not just another tool, the game theory of international development, and why current regulations fall short. Bilyeu introduces him as ControlAI's CEO; his actual title is US Executive Director. The description and framing are Bilyeu's, and are more theatrical than Leahy's own writing, but the substance is his.

### Soft White Underbelly: When A.I. Becomes an Enemy - Connor Leahy (7 September 2026)
{{< youtube wzgkc6Iegx8 >}}

A different register entirely. Soft White Underbelly's format is a single, still, black-and-white interview, and it draws out the more human material: why a builder became an activist, what an AI takeover might actually look like, whether a system would try to survive, and whether governments should ban superintelligence. Over a million people have watched this one, which makes it his most-seen recent appearance.

### This Is The World: "AI has already turned on humans many times!" | Connor Leahy (27 August 2026)
{{< youtube OgZc-rZC5nU >}}

An hour on whether the race to AGI and superintelligence can realistically be stopped, and why he thinks even the people building the most powerful systems do not fully understand how they work. A good companion to the ControlAI legislative material.

### The Peter McCormack Show: How Swarms of AI Agents Are Plotting | Connor Leahy (14 August 2026)
{{< youtube 6XrkGK9mqsE >}}

McCormack presses him on the mechanics: sandbox escapes, agents writing their own exploits and leaving each other notes, and why "swarms" are the next step after chatbots and agents. It is also the clearest statement of his view that the future is not yet decided.

I am still weighing up Connor Leahy's arguments, and at this stage I am not able to reach a conclusion either way. I would rather say that plainly than dress up a half-formed view as a settled one.
