## TL;DR

- **OpenAI** launched **GPT-6 Astra** on 3 September, its first model rated "Critical" for cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework
- **Nvidia** agreed to buy **Hugging Face** for about **$13 billion** on 3 September
- **DeepSeek** released **V4.1 Flash** in September
- **Anthropic** released **Claude Opus 5.5** on 22 September
- **Google** announced **Gemini 4 Argon** on 30 September, with initial access limited to cyber defenders

Performance claims below come from the companies unless noted.

## GPT-6 Astra (3 September)

According to [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/openai-launches-gpt-6-astra) and [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-openai-gpt-6-astra-agi-era-2026), OpenAI launched GPT-6 Astra with a staged rollout, starting with customers in its gated Daybreak enterprise program before broader access. It is the first OpenAI model designated at the "Critical" cybersecurity threshold under the Preparedness Framework. OpenAI's own benchmark figures put it ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Fable 5.1 on a computer-use test, and Greg Brockman described the launch as the start of an "AGI era", which is a framing rather than a measured result. Pricing figures vary between sources.

## Nvidia to Buy Hugging Face (3 September)

Nvidia announced it will buy Hugging Face for about $12.93 billion, according to [Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/09/03/2026/nvidia-to-buy-hugging-face-for-nearly-13-billion) and [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/nvidia-spend-13-billion-hugging-face-leaving-open-136169595):

- About $11.9 billion goes to existing investors and shareholders, with roughly $1 billion set aside as an employee retention package
- Jensen Huang wrote that Hugging Face will remain an open platform and that Nvidia compute will not be required to build on or deploy through it
- Closing is expected in the first half of 2027, pending regulatory approval

## DeepSeek V4.1 Flash

DeepSeek released V4.1 Flash, a model built for efficiency and low price. I covered its pricing in [Own the Agent, Rent the Intelligence](/ai/mac-mini-m6-always-on-ai-agent-server/).

## Claude Opus 5.5 (22 September)

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 as the first model in a Claude 5.5 family. According to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/anthropic-releases-opus-5-5-with-lower-prices-and-fable-level-performance/) and [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/anthropic-launches-claude-opus-5-5-with-fable-5-1-level-performance-at-a-40-lower-price/):

- Anthropic says it performs near Claude Fable 5.1 on most work at about 40% lower cost than Opus 5, with reported pricing of $4 and $20 per million input and output tokens. Reported figures differ between outlets
- It launches with safeguards similar to Fable 5.1 because Anthropic rates it comparable in biology and cybersecurity
- Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 followed, with Haiku 5.5 arriving on 7 October, covered in the [October roundup](/ai/ai-roundup-october-2026/)

## Gemini 4 Argon (30 September)

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon, which went first to trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind program, with paid API customers and AI Ultra subscribers to follow. See [TheNextWeb's report](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-gemini-4-argon-cyber-defenders-fairwind). Google says it is built for long-running work, and its benchmark claims have not been independently verified. The rollout is covered further in the [October roundup](/ai/ai-roundup-october-2026/).
