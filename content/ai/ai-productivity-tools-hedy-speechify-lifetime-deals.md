## TL;DR

- **Voice is the best way to prompt AI agents**: hold a hotkey, talk, and you give more context, think of more as you go, and move much faster than typing
- **Hedy AI** is a real-time conversation coach, not just a note taker. It suggests what to say while the meeting is happening
- Hedy has a **lifetime plan**: $299 on its own site, and [$179 on AppSumo](https://appsumo.com/products/hedy-ai/) at the time I checked
- **MacWhisper** is the one-time-purchase option: a free version, plus Pro for a one-off €64 with lifetime updates
- **Speechify** has no lifetime plan. Its pricing page shows free and Premium only
- **Otter, Fathom and Granola** do not list lifetime options either. All three are subscription only
- The only Speechify route on AppSumo I found was a **one-year** bundle, and it was sold out

## Why this category is worth a look

Most AI productivity tools fall into two groups. One group turns speech into text: meeting notes, transcripts, dictation. The other turns text into speech so you can listen instead of read. Hedy sits in the first group with a twist. Speechify is the best known name in the second.

The catch is that almost all of them are subscriptions. If you stack three or four, the monthly cost adds up quickly, which is why the lifetime question matters.

## The best use of all of this: talk to your AI agents

Before the individual tools, the one habit that has changed how I work with AI. This section is my own opinion and experience, not reported fact.

Dictation apps are the quiet winner of this whole category, and the way you use them is almost embarrassingly simple. You pick a hotkey, you hold it down, you talk, and you let go. Your words land in whatever app has focus: a chat window, a terminal, a coding agent. Most of these apps let you change the hotkey to whatever suits your keyboard and hands, so it ends up being muscle memory within a day or two. That is the whole workflow.

I think this is a much better way to write prompts for AI agents than typing them, for three reasons:

- **You give far more context.** Typing is effortful, so we compress. We write "fix the login bug" when what we mean is three sentences about what is broken, what we already tried and what we do not want touched. Speaking removes the friction, so the background comes out naturally. Agents do better with that background
- **Speaking generates ideas.** Talking a problem through is a form of thinking. Halfway through a sentence you notice an edge case, a constraint or a better approach, and you add it on the spot. Typed prompts tend to stop at the first thing you thought of. Spoken ones keep going
- **It is much faster.** The best-known number here is a [2016 Stanford study](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2016/08/stanford-study-speech-recognition-faster-texting), which found speech recognition was about three times faster than typing on a phone for English, with a lower error rate. That study was on phone keyboards, not a full-size desktop one, and the tools have changed a lot since, so read it as a rough guide. Even so, anyone who has dictated a long prompt knows how big the gap feels

Messy speech used to be the catch. Several of the newer apps clean up the ums, restarts and mid-sentence changes of direction for you. My [dictation stack comparison](/ai/mac-dictation-tools-comparison/) goes into which apps do this best, and the [MacWhisper](#macwhisper) section below covers one that does system-wide dictation. Check the settings of whichever you pick to see how its hotkey works, because hold-to-talk and customisable keys vary from app to app.

A practical tip: dictate the first draft of the prompt, read it back once, and send it. Do not tidy it into something short. The rambling version usually gets a better result from the agent.

## Hedy AI

[Hedy](https://www.hedy.ai/) describes itself as an AI conversation coach. Its site says it provides real-time suggestions, talking points and follow-up questions during meetings, interviews and other conversations, and also captures transcripts and action items. It lists support for 30+ languages, and apps for iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, web and Apple Watch.

The privacy claims are a big part of the pitch. Per Hedy's own site, audio does not leave your device in the default mode, it does not train models on your data, and it lists SOC 2 Type I certification, a HIPAA assessment and an EU data residency option. Those are the company's claims, and I have not audited them.

Outside reviews are broadly positive on the real-time angle. The [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hedy-ai-meeting-coach-notes/id6502325255) is where the search results showed a rating of around 4.8 stars, and a [review roundup](https://www.loudegg.com/hedy-review/) is another place to read opinions. Some reviewers reported the app failing to launch on Mac and occasional glitches in noisy rooms, so treat it like any young app and test it on your own setup before relying on it for an important call.

### Hedy pricing

From [Hedy's pricing page](https://www.hedy.ai/pricing/):

| Plan | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | Up to 5 hours a month, 30+ languages, summaries and meeting notes, real-time AI insights for the first 30 minutes of each session |
| Pro | $12.99/month or $99.99/year | Unlimited meeting length and sessions, import and transcribe past recordings, organise and connect across sessions, Zapier, Make and API integrations |
| Lifetime | $299 one-time | Everything in Pro, no recurring fees, all future updates |
| Teams | Preview | Shared workspaces, team-wide Topics and centralised seat management. Waitlist only when I checked |

### The AppSumo lifetime deal

This is the interesting part. The [AppSumo listing for Hedy AI](https://appsumo.com/products/hedy-ai/) offers lifetime access to the Pro plan for **$179 once**, which it presents as 40% off the regular $299. As listed:

- It is a one-time payment, and the deal is not stackable
- You need to redeem within 60 days of purchase
- It includes all future Pro plan updates
- It is refundable for 60 days under AppSumo's guarantee
- Listed features include real-time coaching in 30+ languages, post-session summaries, data export in Markdown, CSV or JSON, and integrations with Zapier, n8n, webhooks, a REST API and an MCP server

One thing to read carefully: the listing frames the price as "$179 once vs $1,188/yr". That yearly figure is much higher than the $99.99 annual Pro price on Hedy's own site, so the "savings" headline is flattering. The honest comparison is $179 once against $99.99 a year, which means the lifetime deal pays for itself in under two years if you stay a Pro user. Whether that is a good bet depends on whether Hedy is still around and still good in 2028. Lifetime deals are a bet on the company as much as the product. AppSumo deals also come and go, so check the page for current availability.

## Speechify

[Speechify](https://speechify.com/) is the text-to-speech tool: paste in an article, PDF or document and it reads it aloud, with natural-sounding AI voices. I covered it in more detail in [my earlier Speechify post](/ai/speechify/). Its [current pricing page](https://speechify.com/pricing/) shows:

- **Free:** $0. Listening at up to 1.5x, 10 robotic voices, text-to-speech only
- **Premium:** $29/month, or yearly billing that the page says saves 60%. You get 1000+ natural voices, 60+ languages, up to 5x listening speed, Scan & Listen, AI summaries and chat, cloud integrations (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive), voice typing, AI podcasts and a voice AI assistant
- **Speechify Studio:** a separate product for professional voiceovers and video dubbing
- **Speechify API:** a separate product for developers who want to build the voices into their own apps

The pricing page doesn't state the annual price outright, and third-party sources disagree on it, so check the toggle on the page.

### Can you get Speechify for life?

**No, not from Speechify.** The pricing page has no lifetime plan. Some third-party sites advertise "Speechify lifetime deals", but they appear to be aggregators or resellers, not official offers, and I would not hand money to them.

The closest thing on AppSumo was the [AI Studio Bundle](https://appsumo.com/products/ai-studio-bundle/): $39 for **one year** of Fathom Premium, HeyGen Creator and Speechify Studio Basic. That is not a lifetime deal. The page says it is non-refundable and that each tool renews at its standard annual price after 12 months. It was also sold out when I checked. Note too that this bundle covers Speechify **Studio** (voiceovers), which is a different product from the consumer reading app.

## MacWhisper

[MacWhisper](https://www.macwhisper.com/) is a Mac-only transcription and dictation app, and it is the odd one out in this list because it is a one-time purchase, not a subscription. It is sold direct via Gumroad and on the App Store. I compared it with Wispr Flow and Superwhisper in my [dictation stack post](/ai/mac-dictation-tools-comparison/).

According to the [MacWhisper site](https://www.macwhisper.com/), there are two versions:

| Version | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Free | €0 | Basic transcription, mic recording, common audio and video formats (mp3, wav, m4a, ogg, opus, mov, mp4), 100+ languages, export to .txt, .srt and .vtt |
| Pro | €64 one-time, lifetime updates included | Everything in Free, plus advanced export (.md, .pdf, .html, .docx), speaker recognition, YouTube, URL and podcast transcription, batch processing, translation, system-wide dictation, grammar improvement, video subtitles, meeting recording (Zoom, Teams, Webex and others), AI service integration, watched-folder automation, workflow automation (Notion, Zapier and others) and CLI access |

The AI service integration list on the site covers OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Google Gemini, Ollama, DeepSeek, Azure, LM Studio, OpenRouter, Deepgram, ElevenLabs and Gladia, so you can plug in your own provider for clean-up and summaries. The price shown to me was in euros, and the figure in my older post was in dollars, so expect the number you see at checkout to depend on your region and the storefront.

There is no lifetime "deal" here, because the normal price is already a one-off. That makes it the cheapest long-term option in this post for anyone on a Mac.

## Where lifetime deals do exist

If you want to own the tool outright, the options I found are mostly on-device apps and smaller names:

- **Dictation and transcription on Mac:** MacWhisper Pro is a one-time purchase (see above), and Superwhisper has a lifetime tier. Both are covered in my [dictation stack comparison](/ai/mac-dictation-tools-comparison/). The logic is simple: if the compute happens on your machine, the vendor has no ongoing inference bill, so a one-off price is sustainable
- **AppSumo listings:** a search of AppSumo turned up lifetime deals for several smaller voice and meeting tools, including [WriteVoice](https://appsumo.com/products/writevoice/), [Letterly](https://appsumo.com/products/letterly/), [Hyprcore](https://appsumo.com/products/hyprcore/) and [Laxis](https://appsumo.com/products/laxis/). I have not tested any of them, so this is a pointer, not a recommendation

## My read

This section is opinion, not reporting.

- If you are in lots of calls, interviews or negotiations and want live prompting, Hedy is the most distinctive tool here, and the $179 lifetime deal is the cheapest way I found to try it with a 60-day refund window as a safety net
- If you just want recordings and summaries for free, Fathom's free tier is hard to beat
- If you are on a Mac and want transcription and dictation without a subscription, MacWhisper Pro's one-off price is the simplest answer
- If you want to listen to your reading list, Speechify is the one to beat, but you will be paying a subscription for it
- Treat any lifetime deal as a bet on the company surviving. Pay what you would be happy to lose, and use the refund window to test it properly

Prices and availability change often. Check each page before you buy.
