## *On Effort, Tests, and Choosing What to Pay Attention To*

---

*A note before you read: this is a personal essay, not a claim about how the world demonstrably works. The ideas here about a test, a next level, and something that answers when you do the work are my own beliefs as they stand today, held loosely. I say more about that at the end.*

I have become convinced, over a few years rather than in one clean moment, that the Universe rewards us for putting the work in. That sounds like the sort of thing you read on a fridge magnet, so let me be more precise about what I mean. It is not that effort is magically repaid. It is that the act of starting seems to open something. The moment I suggest a direction and actually move on it - send the message, sketch the thing, write the first paragraph - a whole flood of new connections seems to follow. Ideas that were invisible while I sat still become obvious the moment I am in motion.

The practical consequence is that there are always more possibilities than I can keep up with. That is a strange kind of problem to have, but it is a real one: not a shortage of ideas, but the discipline to choose among a surplus of them.

## Life as one big test

I genuinely believe that life is one big test. Whether we are living in a physical universe or some kind of simulation, I am honestly not too bothered. I have written before about [how likely it is that we are living in a simulation](/theories/how-likely-are-we-living-in-a-simulation/), and my honest answer remains that nobody knows. But I have noticed that the question matters less to how I live than I once assumed. Either way I would rather enjoy the ride.

Where I have landed is this: I would rather take the gamble that we are being tested and judged, and that when our time here ends we are assessed on what we learned and how we handled things. Maybe we move on to the next level if we have shown we are ready. Maybe we repeat the same level until we get it right. I cannot prove either, and I hold it as a belief rather than a fact, but it is a genuinely interesting way to look at a life. It makes an ordinary Tuesday feel like it counts for something.

## The distractions are part of the challenge

If life is a test, then it makes sense that there would be things designed to take you off the task. I think there are plenty of intentional distractions on Earth, and the negative ones are the most effective, because they are the ones that quietly drain you without ever asking for your consent. Doomscrolling. Outrage. Arguments that go nowhere. The slow bleed of attention into things that leave you emptier than they found you.

Part of the challenge, I think, is learning to recognise those and step around them, and to spend the energy on what you can actually contribute instead. This is easier to write than to do - the negative pulls are engineered to be hard to resist - but the direction keeps proving itself when I manage it.

## Persistence, and not chasing the money

I believe that anyone willing to put the effort in, stay persistent and refuse to give up has a real chance of succeeding and eventually getting what they want. That is not a novel insight - it is what you hear over and over from people who have built something, and it is almost boringly consistent across the ones worth listening to. The lesson underneath it is unglamorous: keep going when it is difficult, and do not abandon your ideas at the first sign that they are hard.

The part people repeat less often is the rest of it. Do not make money the primary focus at the beginning. Concentrate on creating something genuinely valuable, and the money can follow when the timing is right. I find that ordering matters. Chasing the money first tends to distort the thing you are building until it is no longer worth the money you were chasing. Creating value first, and letting the money catch up, keeps the work honest.

## The part I cannot reconcile

The one thing I still struggle to understand is why there is so much suffering in the world, particularly when some people experience terrible illnesses or circumstances through no apparent fault of their own. If there is a test, or a curriculum, or a plan with a shape to it, this is the part that does not fit neatly against it. I do not have an answer that satisfies me. I have read the attempts - lessons, growth through hardship, something we cannot see from here - and none of them fully hold up when you are looking at real pain in a real person who did nothing to deserve it.

I would rather say that plainly than paper over it with a comfortable theory. It is the honest edge of the whole idea, and I would rather leave it standing there as a genuine open question than pretend it resolves.

## What I can control, and what I have stopped feeding

Over the same period, I have come to realise how little control I actually have over politics, wars and the monetary system. I can vote, but I cannot control the decisions politicians make, who goes to war with whom, or how the global financial system operates. For a long time I gave these things a great deal of my attention. It felt like being informed. Looking at it honestly, it was closer to carrying weight I could never put down.

So I made a conscious decision to stop. Not out of indifference - I still care, and I still form views - but I no longer feed these subjects the hours of the day that they want. A lot of my friends want to focus on them, and I understand why. But I have noticed that constantly discussing them drains my positive energy without actually changing any of the outcomes, and I would rather put that energy into things I *can* influence: building things, exploring ideas, helping people, and seeing what we can make happen.

I am not burying my head in the sand. I am choosing where to spend a finite amount of attention, and I would rather spend it where it can do something. That is not disengagement. It is a decision about where the energy goes.

A caveat worth being honest about: all of this is where I am right now, not a fixed position. I revise it as I read more and as life tests the edges of it. I hold these ideas loosely and expect them to move, some of them sooner than I would like. That looseness is the point - it is the only way I know to stay close to something true.

## A final thought

The Universe, as best I can tell, does not respond to the people who wait. It responds to the ones who move, even badly, even early, even without the money worked out yet. The work seems to be what opens the door.

The rest - the tests, the next level, the parts that do not reconcile - I hold as questions rather than answers. But the part I am sure of is the small one underneath all of it: focus on what you can control, put the work in, and make the most of the time you have got here.
